# Last Bell High: map design

How the whole school should play once every area exists, and the checklist each
new room has to pass. The rules come from what makes the best Black Ops 1 and 2
maps work (Kino der Toten, Der Riese, Mob of the Dead, Origins) and what makes
the weakest ones frustrating (Five, TranZit, Die Rise).

## What the classics get right

1. **Two paths that loop.** Kino der Toten's two routes are linked to one
   another around the theater, which gives several ways to play the map
   depending on which doors you open. Der Riese splits at the start into two
   1000-point paths (the garage side and the animal lab side) that join up again
   at the middle of the map.
2. **Big, open training spots, especially in the late game.** Kino's stage
   opens into a wide space once the power is on, and the theater is where you
   run trains in later rounds. Der Riese's courtyard and Mob of the Dead's docks
   and Broadway play the same role. What they share: one large space with an
   obstacle in the middle to circle around, lanes wide enough to slip past a
   zombie, and more than one way out.
3. **Every area gives you a reason to go there.** A perk, a wall weapon, a box
   spot, a trap, the power, a part for something, or a step of the Easter egg.
   Mob of the Dead scatters its plane parts across the warden's office, docks,
   tunnels, showers and roof. Origins spreads generators, dig spots and staff
   parts across the whole map, so players keep coming back to areas.
4. **Setup is short and readable.** Power and the upgrade machine are a
   journey, but not a chore. TranZit is criticised for having power and
   Pack-a-Punch far from spawn and needing to be built first.
5. **No cramped, dead-end mazes.** Five is criticised for cramped rooms and
   hallways that are hard to run through with a horde behind you. Tight spaces
   are fine as connectors and tense moments, but they shouldn't be where you
   have to survive.
6. **Landmarks you can read at a glance.** The Kino theater and the Der Riese
   mainframe tell you where you are. Each of our rooms needs one landmark
   object.
7. **Traps at chokepoints.** Kino and Der Riese use electric traps on doorways
   and bridges. Paying to shut a corridor turns a choke point into a weapon.

## Rules for every area we add

- **A reason to come back.** At least one item from: perk, wall weapon, box
  spot, trap, power, Mad Dog Machine, or a hidden lore or Easter egg item.
- **A landmark.** One object you can recognise from across the room.
- **No pure dead ends.** A room with one exit needs something valuable inside,
  so that being cornered is a risk you choose to take.
- **Escape routes.** At least two ways out of anything bigger than a closet.
- **Windows facing outdoors.** Zombies come in from outside the building.
  Outdoor play areas instead have climb-over fences and ground spawns, and any
  window that would face into a playable area gets removed.
- **Prices go up with depth.** 750 near spawn, about 1000 to 1250 mid-map,
  1250 to 1500 for the deepest rooms.

## The layout we're building toward

```
                          AUDITORIUM  (both paths meet; Mad Dog Machine,
                         /    |    \   perk, box spot, late-game trains)
            band / art room   |   loading dock & back lot (bus, box spot)
                  |           |            |
              LIBRARY         |          kitchen
                  |           |            |
            science lab       |        CAFETERIA ──────┐
                  |           |            |            |
            locker rooms ── COURT ──── HALLWAY ──── THE QUAD  (new courtyard)
                  |        (spawn)         |            |
            boiler room                FRONT OFFICE ───┘
            (power, down
             the stairs)
```

- **West path:** court, locker rooms, science lab, library, band and art rooms,
  then the auditorium. The boiler room, with the power switch, is a short
  branch down the stairs from the locker rooms.
- **East path:** court, hallway, cafeteria, kitchen, loading dock, then the
  auditorium.
- **The big loop:** the two paths meet at the auditorium, so you can run a full
  circle around the school.
- **The Quad's small loop:** the hallway, cafeteria, Quad and front office link
  up into a mid-game loop, so you don't need the whole map open to have
  somewhere to run.

## The Quad (new, added in Phase 4)

An outdoor courtyard east of the main hallway, about 18 m by 36 m. It's built
for running trains.

- **Doors in:** from the main hallway (about 1000), the cafeteria's south wall
  (about 1250), and the front office (about 1250). Three exits, so you can't get
  pinned.
- **What's in it:** a dead fountain on a raised planter with a flagpole as the
  thing you circle (roughly 8 m across), lanes at least 5 m wide around it, low
  benches and planters at the edges only, and moonlight and fog.
- **How zombies arrive:** they climb over the chain-link fence on the open
  east side and rise out of the dirt planters at the corners. Spawns stay near
  the edges so the loop around the fountain stays clean.
- **What to do there:** a Mystery Box spot (a fifth one, added to the four in
  the spec), a wall weapon, and later one Easter egg item. The Quad also links
  the office back into the rest of the map, so the office stops being a dead
  end.
- **What it replaces:** the hallway's east windows and the cafeteria's south
  window face the Quad, so they get removed once the Quad exists. The broken
  school bus I put in the courtyard moves to the loading dock, where the spec
  places it.

## Proposed extras (not in the original spec)

These are classic features that would give rooms more to do. They need a
yes before they get built.

- **Electric traps** (once the power is on): in the kitchen and locker room
  doorways, for about 1000 points each, with a cooldown.
- **Hidden Easter egg song:** find three Stew items around the school to play a
  Stew song that's generated in code (Phase 8).
- **Erik's intercom:** using the PA in the principal's office makes Erik answer
  (Phase 8).
